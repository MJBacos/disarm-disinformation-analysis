Comparative Analysis: Thailand vs. U.S. Domestic Disinformation Campaigns

This file presents a comparative analysis between Thailand's state-sponsored disinformation campaigns (as observed in the DISARM dataset) and domestic disinformation efforts in the United States, particularly those surrounding the Trump administration and the 2020 election cycle.

Overview

While Thailand’s campaigns were operated by the Royal Thai Army to control internal dissent, similar disinformation behaviors emerged in the United States — not from foreign actors, but from domestic political operatives, influencers, and hyper-partisan networks.

Both cases demonstrate that disinformation is a technique, not exclusive to authoritarian regimes.

Shared Characteristics

Manufactured Consensus: Both used mass engagement (real or fake) to simulate popular support.

Platform Abuse: Exploited Twitter, Facebook, and YouTube algorithms to drive reach.

Nationalism: Promoted narratives equating the regime (Thailand) or figurehead (Trump) with national identity.

Discrediting Dissent: Protesters and journalists were framed as enemies of the state.

Strategic Difference

Thailand: Centralized, military-run operation targeting domestic legitimacy.

United States: Decentralized but coordinated campaigns driven by political influencers, media outlets, and grassroots supporters — aimed at delegitimizing elections, institutions, and opposition.

Conclusion

Despite differences in structure and origin, both the Thailand and U.S. campaigns reveal how disinformation can:

Manipulate public opinion

Undermine democratic processes

Blur the line between truth and tribal loyalty

By using frameworks like DISARM, we can map, compare, and better understand the tactics behind influence operations — whether they originate from a military junta or a political campaign rally.
